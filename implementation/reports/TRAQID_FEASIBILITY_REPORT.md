## 1. Executive Summary & Purpose

This report provides a formal technical audit of the **Traffic-Related Air Quality Image Dataset (TRAQID)** developed by researchers at the International Institute of Information Technology, Hyderabad (IIIT-H) (Kathalkar et al., ACM ICVGIP 2024), examining its feasibility for integration into the stationary Continuous Ambient Air Quality Monitoring Station (CAAQMS) hourly forecasting framework.

### Technical Assessment Outcome
**VERDICT: TRAQID CANNOT BE MERGED INTO CPCB HOURLY TIME-SERIES.**  
TRAQID must be maintained strictly as an **independent, quarantined auxiliary computer vision and mobile exposure case study**. Under no circumstances should its artificial timestamps or mobile sensor traces be synthesized or merged into the continuous CPCB ground-truth hourly records.

## 2. In-Depth Inspection of the TRAQID Repository & Dataset Architecture

### 2.1 Repository Contents
Inspection of `reference_repositories/TRAQID-Traffic-Related-Air-Quality-Image-Dataset` revealed:
- `README.md` (detailed documentation, methodology, sensor descriptions, author warning).
- `LICENSE` (MIT License).
- Raw image data and tabular CSVs are hosted externally on `india-data.org` and Google Drive due to volume (26,678 image pairs, multiple gigabytes).

### 2.2 Dataset Attributes and Data Collection Setup
- **Sample Count:** 26,678 paired front and rear dashboard camera images (640 $\times$ 360 resolution).
- **Vehicular Platform:** Instrumented passenger car driving ~2,000 km across Hyderabad and Secunderabad over **70+ driving hours across 20 sporadic days** between October 2022 and July 2024.
- **Sensors:**
  - Front camera: DDPAI Mola N3 (2688 $\times$ 1944, 30 fps).
  - Rear camera: DDPAI X2 Pro (1920 $\times$ 1080, 30 fps).
  - Air Quality Node: Custom ESP32 with Nova SDS011 optical particle counter (PM2.5, PM10) and Bosch BME280 (temperature, relative humidity) sampled at 5-second intervals.
  - Reference Calibration: Calibrated against an Aeroqual S500 portable monitor using linear regression.

## 3. Critical Feasibility Findings

### 3.1 Unrecoverable CSV Timestamps (Author Disclaimer)
The authors of TRAQID explicitly document a critical limitation regarding the temporal column in `README.md`:

> *"During preprocessing, the sensor streams (logged at different native rates) were resampled and interpolated onto a common 5-second grid so that each image frame could be assigned a measurement. In that step, the `Timestamp` column was regenerated as a uniform index for the resampled series. **It does not hold the original capture time of each image.**"*  
> *"As a result, CSV timestamps will not match the date and time overlaid on the images, and the gap between them is not constant across the dataset."*  
> *"Use the timestamp overlaid on each image as the actual capture time. Do not use the CSV `Timestamp` column for time-based analysis."*

**Impact on Hourly Forecasting:**
1. The tabular timestamps in `TRAQID.csv` are mathematical counter artifacts generated by uniform 5-second resampling.
2. Synchronizing TRAQID rows with CPCB hourly timestamps (which follow strict Indian Standard Time) would require performing Optical Character Recognition (OCR) on burned-in video timestamps across 26,678 front/rear image pairs.
3. Even if OCR were applied, the data spans only 20 discrete driving days over a 22-month window. It contains zero coverage for over 95% of calendar dates between 2023 and 2026.

### 3.2 Total Absence of Continuous GPS Navigation Logs
Inspection of the tabular schema confirmed:
- Columns in `TRAQID.csv`: `['ID', 'PM2.5', 'PM10', 'AQI', 'AQI category', 'temperature', 'humidity', 'season', 'Sequence', 'Timestamp']`.
- **Zero geographic coordinates (latitude, longitude) are logged in the CSV.**
- The trajectory data cannot be matched to specific road segments, distance to CAAQMS stations, or OpenStreetMap road density buffers without manual visual route reconstruction or external unreleased GPS tracks.

### 3.3 Micro-Environmental Incompatibility: Mobile Tailpipe Wake vs. Stationary Urban Canopy
1. **Physical Altitude & Exposure:** CPCB CAAQMS sampling inlets are mounted on building rooftops (3 to 15 metres above ground level) in compliance with CPCB siting criteria to measure ambient urban background exposure. In contrast, the Nova SDS011 on the TRAQID car was mounted at ~1.5 metres above the road surface, directly exposed to the turbulent, hot exhaust plumes of preceding diesel trucks and three-wheelers.
2. **Dynamic Range & Volatility:** On-road PM2.5 readings in TRAQID fluctuate drastically on 5-second timescales (spiking from 20 to 350 $\mu\text{g/m}^3$ within seconds behind accelerating heavy vehicles). Averaging these localized micro-plumes into stationary hourly grid nodes would inject spurious variance unrepresentative of regional air masses.

### Scholarly Role as an Auxiliary Case Study
Rather than being discarded, TRAQID will be incorporated in the academic capstone thesis as an **independent auxiliary mobile case study**:
- Demonstrating the physical divergence between mobile near-source commuter exposure and regulatory rooftop background measurements.
- Benchmarking computer vision estimation techniques (e.g., AQC-Net image-based PM2.5 regression) against stationary sensor models.
- Documenting why multi-modal urban datasets must be critically audited for temporal resampling artifacts before machine learning deployment.
